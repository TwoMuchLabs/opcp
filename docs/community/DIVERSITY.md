# Organisational diversity roadmap

GOVERNANCE.md sets two goals:
- before version 1.0, no single organisation employs more than half of the maintainers (section 4);
- once maintainers from at least three organisations are active, the founders contribute the project to a neutral foundation (section 5).

This roadmap breaks the way there into steps that can be checked.

## Where the project stands (7 November 2026)

- Two maintainers, both listed in GOVERNANCE.md. Their organisational affiliations are for the maintainers to record here.
- One implementation (the Python reference runtime).
- No external review round completed yet.

## Steps

| Step | Done when | Why it comes here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Make affiliations visible | Each maintainer's employer and any commercial interest in OPCP is listed in this file | The goal can't be measured without it |
| 2. First external review round | Round 1 of [EXTERNAL-REVIEW.md](EXTERNAL-REVIEW.md) closed and summarised | Brings in people from other organisations at low commitment |
| 3. A second implementation by someone outside the founding organisations | It passes the shared conformance fixtures (#40) | The strongest proof that OPCP doesn't depend on its creators, and the most likely source of a future maintainer |
| 4. First reviewer from another organisation | A reviewer role held by someone outside the founders' organisations ([MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md)) | A step towards maintainership that doesn't need a big commitment |
| 5. Section 4 met | No organisation employs more than half the maintainers | GOVERNANCE.md, before 1.0 |
| 6. Foundation conversation | Maintainers from three organisations active; a public issue opened to choose a foundation, FINOS being the stated candidate | GOVERNANCE.md section 5 |

## What the maintainers will not do to meet these numbers

- Count people who don't do the work. A maintainer is someone who reviews and decides, not a name on a list.
- Split one organisation's people into separate entities to pass the test.
